## TL;DR

CTR restricts where a local contact model can be trusted using physical feasibility of predicted contact forces. Its relaxed variant supports low-compute in-hand and bimanual manipulation by excluding impossible pulling predictions.

## Problem

Pushing and pulling are not interchangeable in contact-rich manipulation. Differentiable simulators make local optimization tractable, but a conventional ellipsoidal trust region (ETR) can admit physically impossible contact-force predictions. Explicitly optimizing all contact modes and complementarity constraints is computationally difficult. This paper asks where a local contact model can be trusted, and uses contact feasibility to answer that question.

## Core idea

When a finger pushes a box, withdrawing the finger does not pull the box along. CTR restores that asymmetry to the local model.

- **Differentiate both state and force**: obtain sensitivities of next-state primal variables and contact-force dual variables from the convex quasidynamic contact (CQDC) model.
- **CTR**: intersect an ellipsoidal region with feasibility constraints on linearized primal and dual predictions, including friction constraints.
- **R-CTR**: drop the potentially overconservative primal feasibility constraint while retaining dual feasibility of predicted contact forces.

<Collapsible title="Method details">

CQDC computes the next robot/object configuration using convex optimization over geometry and friction. Log-barrier smoothing and implicit differentiation provide local sensitivities of both configurations and contact forces. CTR reinstates feasibility conditions omitted by a bare linearization, yielding convex constraints. R-CTR keeps only the dual conditions as a practical relaxation.

MPC optimizes a trajectory inside this local representation, applies the first action, and replans. Initial-guess heuristics mitigate the gap between smoothed planning dynamics and actual nonsmooth contact. Execution under second-order dynamics also requires practical changes such as planning a short future trajectory before executing and replanning. Low offline compute therefore should not be read as high-frequency online control.

Local MPC cannot reliably discover long-horizon moves that first break contact and reposition fingers. The authors sample initial grasps favorable for a target object motion, then connect grasps using MPC and collision-free hand repositioning. A global roadmap reuses these connections, exploiting the cube's 24 rotational symmetries.

## Main results

- **Local CQDC planning, Table 3**: mean bimanual translation error drops from ETR's **8.6 to 2.0 mm**, and rotation error from **9.6 to 2.1 mrad** with R-CTR. Allegro rotation error falls from **21.4 to 9.8 mrad**. These are evaluations within the planning model, not hardware errors.
- **Hardware feedback, Table 6**: closed-loop versus open-loop rotation error improves from **0.056 to 0.024 rad** for IiwaBimanual and **0.323 to 0.258 rad** for AllegroHand. Allegro translation error increases from 0.014 to 0.018 m, so the improvement is not uniform across metrics.
- **Roadmap, Section 8**: the cube roadmap takes **under 10 minutes** on a standard laptop CPU. Hardware executes **150 consecutive edge transitions** before the hand overheats; online inference takes a few seconds. This is a sequence on one constructed roadmap, not 150 arbitrary objects or universal task success.

## Achievements and limitations

Achievements:

- Links the validity region of differentiable contact models to geometric and force feasibility, exposing the unilateral structure lost by symmetric trust regions.
- Reuses that representation for local MPC, grasp synthesis and roadmap planning, with bimanual and in-hand hardware demonstrations.
- Establishes a practical model-based route to contact-rich planning alongside computationally intensive RL. Expert-data synthesis and RL exploration are discussed as future integrations.

Limitations:

- **Author-noted**: quasidynamic predictions still differ from second-order dynamics. Slip and lost contact remain; constraints and heuristics reduce these failures without fully explaining them.
- Global connections rely heavily on Allegro kinematics and cube symmetry. Asymmetric objects or unstable poses require explicit stability reasoning during hand repositioning, such as finger gaiting.
- The roles of feasibility constraints and rare planner failures remain incompletely understood. Exploiting sparsity and pruning inactive contact constraints are proposed speed improvements.
- 🤖 The compute savings are promising, but general superiority over RL is not established by a comparison matched for sensing, object diversity and online latency.
