## TL;DR

RECIPE combines model calibration, contact-and-object rewards, specialist distillation, and depth-position observations to transfer humanoid manipulation from simulation. Brief human initialization states guide exploration; successful RL experience then trains the deployed visual policy.

[Supplied paper v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.20396v1) · [Project and videos](https://toruowo.github.io/recipe/)

## Problem

Dexterous manipulation combines inaccurate robot models, complex contacts, difficult exploration, and visual domain shift. Success on a single skill does not establish a reusable pipeline for bimanual tasks. This work addresses those four bottlenecks together. Evaluation uses the Fourier GR1 arms and hands for grasp-and-reach, box lifting, and handover.

## Core Idea

- **Calibrate the model** by comparing hardware joint tracking with candidate simulated physics and URDF parameters.
- **Structure rewards** around finger contact locations and target object states.
- **Simplify exploration and distill** using brief human initialization states and successful trajectories from object-group specialists.
- **Combine perception** from an egocentric depth camera and externally observed 3D object position.

<Figure src="./fig-2.png" caption="Figure 2: Four components of the recipe: model calibration, contact/object rewards, specialist training and distillation, and hybrid visual observations." number={2} />

<Collapsible title="Method Details">

**Autotune.** Sample parameter candidates around the manufacturer model, execute matching joint targets in hardware and parallel simulation, and select the lowest tracking-error MSE. Objects use randomized geometric primitives. This calibrates robot behavior; RialTo and SimFoundry place greater emphasis on reconstructing deployment scenes.

**Contact and object goals.** Surface markers specify desired fingertip contacts, while object-position targets specify movement. Handover switches the active hand and goal by task stage. Human task structure therefore remains part of the reward.

**PPO specialists → Diffusion Policy.** An asymmetric actor-critic trains specialists with PPO. Successful specialist trajectories are filtered and used to train a deployable Diffusion Policy. Smaller object groups ease exploration, but the fastest simulation learner is not necessarily the best hardware transfer policy.

**Perception and supervision.** SAM2 tracks the object in the external view, with depth providing approximate 3D position; egocentric depth adds shape information. Perception and policy control run at 5 Hz. Full successful demonstrations are unnecessary, but each task still uses under 30 seconds of human play states for initialization. ## Key Results

- **Across-object evaluation**, IV-G: best-performing task policies achieve **62.3% grasp-and-reach**, **80% box lift**, and **52.5% bimanual handover**, averaging ten trials per test object.
- **Perception ablation**, Table III: depth-only grasping and lifting achieve **2/10** and **0/10**, versus **10/10** and **10/10** with depth plus position. Completed handover A/B changes from **0/10 and 0/10 to 9/10 and 5/10**. These ten-trial comparisons are separate from the overall rates above.
- **Initialization ablation**, Table II: human initialization raises the fraction of successful training seeds from **0% to 30% for handover**, and **60% to 80% for grasping**. Success means a learned policy exceeds 60% episodic success, assessed over ten seeds. This is a training-success fraction, not a hardware task-success rate.

<Figure src="./tab-3.png" caption="Table 3: Depth plus object position versus depth alone. Pickup and completed-task counts are distinct; handover is reported as A and B." number={3} />

Model calibration also matters: Table I reports **8/10 grasp and 7/10 reach** with the lowest-MSE model, versus **0/10 for both** with the highest-MSE model. Each setting evaluates ten independently trained checkpoints. [Experiments: IV-C–G](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20396v1#S4)

## Achievements and Limitations

Achievements:

- Validates a connected modeling, reward, learning, and perception pipeline on real humanoid manipulation hardware.
- Uses short initialization data and simulated RL experience to reduce dependence on complete human success trajectories.
- Demonstrates examples of unseen-object and alternative-hand transfer, disturbance tolerance, and regrasping.

Limitations:

- The authors identify conventional dynamics randomization as insufficient for more dynamic interactions, potentially contributing to weaker handover results.
- Better integration of human priors into rewards and more reliable hands with richer active degrees of freedom remain open directions.
- 🤖 **Evaluation scope**: three task families and best-policy results leave general-purpose manipulation unproven. New tasks and camera arrangements need seed-averaged evaluation that also accounts for total human setup effort.

[Author limitations: V](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20396v1#S5). Numbers and figure numbering here follow the supplied v1 PDF.

Together they distinguish hardware calibration, scene-specific adaptation, and environment expansion as separate research problems.
