## TL;DR

RialTo strengthens a demonstration-trained manipulation policy by practicing recovery inside a digital twin of its deployment scene. Inverse distillation connects visual demonstrations to simulator states, and real-data co-training helps the final policy return to hardware.

[Paper v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03949v3) · [Project and videos](https://real-to-sim-to-real.github.io/RialTo/) · [Code](https://github.com/real-to-sim-to-real/RialToPolicyLearning)

## Problem

Behavior cloning cannot readily recover from mistakes absent from its demonstrations. Real-robot RL adds costly exploration and repeated hardware interaction. RialTo targets dependable manipulation in a particular deployment environment. It asks whether reconstructing that environment can supply the extra experience needed for recovery.

## Core Idea

- **Transfer the scene**: scan geometry, separate objects, and annotate joints through a GUI.
- **Transfer demonstrations**: run the real-data visual policy in simulation and attach simulator states to successful trajectories. This is inverse distillation.
- **Improve and deploy**: train a state-based RL policy, then teach a point-cloud student while retaining real demonstrations.

<Collapsible title="Method Details">

A scan does not identify accurate physics. Users organize objects and joints; mass and friction use shared defaults. The twin provides task geometry and interaction structure without claiming complete physical identification.

Inverse distillation does not estimate ground-truth states from recorded real observations. It acquires states during successful simulator executions of the visual policy. Direct human demonstrations in simulation can replace this initialization route.

PPO combines sparse task-success rewards with demonstration supervision. Demonstrations aid exploration and discourage exploiting inaccurate simulated physics. DAgger then transfers the state teacher to a point-cloud student, incorporating real demonstrations and visual distractors. [Method: III-B–D](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03949v3#S3)

## Key Results

- **Six-task averages** under pose randomization / distractors / physical disturbances: BC **25 / 11 / 5% → RialTo 91 / 77 / 75%**, or **+66 / +66 / +70 percentage points**, using the averages reported in IV-A.
- **Book placement**, Table I: 50-demo BC achieves **40 / 30 / 20%**, versus **90 / 70 / 60%** for RialTo. The main RialTo comparison uses **15 simulator demos plus 15 real demos during distillation**. Initialization using real demonstrations alone is assessed separately in IV-C2 and Figure 6.
- **Target-scene reconstruction**, Figure 7: success on the real target drawer is **90%** after training on its twin, versus **10%** after training with four Objaverse drawers. This small asset comparison cannot establish superiority over large-scale generalist training.

Hardware evaluation uses at least ten rollouts per condition and bootstrap standard deviations, selecting each method's best policy. Figure 5 covers six core tasks; v3 describes eight overall, including additional everyday environments. [Experiments: IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03949v3#S4)

## Achievements and Limitations

Achievements:

- Connects reconstructed scenes to further policy learning for physical manipulation.
- Provides a practical route to recovery behaviors such as regrasping and realignment beyond the demonstrations.

Limitations:

- Authors identify depth sensing on thin, transparent, or reflective objects, deformables, and faster dynamic tasks as remaining challenges.
- End-to-end training takes approximately **two days per task**. The GUI study's **14 minutes 40 seconds** of average active work is not total policy-training time.
- 🤖 **Evaluation scope**: few repetitions and best-policy selection do not establish long-term unattended reliability. Seed-averaged, repeated evaluation in new locations remains necessary.

[Author limitations: VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03949v3#S7) · [User study: VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03949v3#S6)
