## TL;DR

A frozen language model can select useful manipulation targets without a pretrained motor policy, but the effect is strongly model-specific. Astra's semantic strengths coexist with a substantial precision and execution gap.

## Problem

Robot systems commonly separate language-based planning from fast learned motor control. Direct LLM control has been demonstrated, but its capability across a complete public benchmark remains unclear. Small qualitative demonstrations also obscure whether semantic understanding translates into reliable contact and coordinated motion. This study evaluates a frozen LLM as the target-selecting policy across RoboDojo and examines where that arrangement fails.

## Key Idea

**LLM as policy** means no learned policy sits between the language model and robot, and the model receives no robot- or task-specific fine-tuning for this evaluation.

- The LLM receives RGB views, robot state, task instructions and interaction history, then selects end-effector targets.
- A non-learned module validates targets, converts poses and plans executable joint paths. This is still a substantial geometric execution stack.
- The study evaluates complete benchmark coverage, demonstration conditioning and selected perturbation responses separately.

### Observation and action loop

Each turn includes three RGB views, 14 grasp-point state dimensions, 12 read-only joint angles and the remaining step budget. Text history is retained, while images are kept for only the latest two observation turns. The prompt includes the official instruction plus a wiki-derived task recipe and scoring stages. It also advises active viewing with an idle wrist camera.

The model calls `move_eef` or `give_up`. Motion targets can specify either or both arms; omitted dimensions retain observed values. Non-learned conversion validates requests, converts grasp-point targets to flange poses and plans joint paths, resampled at 25 Hz. **25 Hz is the path execution rate, not the LLM inference frequency.** Rejected requests produce feedback without motion; accepted requests return an achieved state and target error after execution. The model sees no metric depth, privileged object poses or reward state.

### Evaluation boundary

Astra and GPT-5.5 each run 50 episodes on all 42 tasks; DeepSeek-Flash runs ten per task. All use medium reasoning effort and generally 100 calls per episode, with one evaluation seed. Score measures mean process reward multiplied by 100; SR requires full task success. The leaderboard Average equally weights five axes rather than pooling all episodes. Public policy rows come from a September 10, 2026 snapshot and were not rerun with equivalent wiki-derived text.

### Context probes

The one-shot probe compares the same 340 task–layout pairs across 34 tasks, inserting a demonstration from another layout as images plus EEF poses or as text. The perturbation probe uses eight `general_pickup` layouts already solved without perturbation. Its selected traces can show corrections, but do not isolate adaptation from feedback control, repeated attempts or prior robustness.

## Key Results

- **Overall benchmark**: Astra reaches **28.97 Score / 22.48% Average SR**, versus DM0.5 at **24.90 / 19.34%** (+3.14 percentage points in SR). Astra's 2,100 trials include 472 full successes; pooled Score is 28.72. GPT-5.5 and DeepSeek-Flash reach only 0.88% and 1.92% Average SR, respectively; the latter uses fewer episodes.
- **Uneven capability**: Generalization SR is 30.50% versus DM0.5's 10.95%, but Precision SR is only **4.00% versus 16.75%**. Long-Horizon and Memory also trail DM0.5. Leading aggregate rank does not mean consistently superior execution.
- **One-shot context**: On matched pairs, zero-shot success is **22.9%**, falling to **17.9%** with image/EEF demonstrations and **12.9%** with text demonstrations. This is evidence about the tested insertion protocol, not a general failure of few-shot learning.

The paper also reports selected real-hardware trials, mobile humanoid recordings and piano-controller synthesis. These use separate protocols and do not enter the official simulation ranking. The real-hardware campaign stopped following unsafe actions and equipment damage, with no person injured; the retained 33 trials cannot establish general reliability.

## Achievements and Limitations

### What's New

- A complete 42-task evaluation places three frozen LLM controllers alongside a 40-policy public board.
- Task-level analysis separates semantic/open-ended strengths from precision, dynamics and bimanual weaknesses.
- Matched demonstration tests and perturbation traces distinguish demonstration conditioning from within-episode correction.

### Limitations

- **Author-noted**: One seed and one main simulator; unequal task information relative to public policies; a smaller DeepSeek-Flash evaluation; selected perturbation layouts; incomplete real-world evaluation. Closed-model benchmark contamination remains unquantified.
- **Author-noted**: RGB-only sensing, the EEF target interface, context truncation and latency may explain some failures. No history ablation establishes a learning mechanism, and qualitative examples establish occurrence rather than frequency.
- 🤖 A matched comparison of task information, wall-clock time and inference budget is needed to determine whether the aggregate lead offers an operational advantage over pretrained policies.
